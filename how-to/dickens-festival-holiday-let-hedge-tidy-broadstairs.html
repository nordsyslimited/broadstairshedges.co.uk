Kerb-appeal hedge work for Broadstairs holiday-let owners.

Broadstairs has plenty of holiday lets, and for those owners the front hedge is part of the first impression a guest gets. The trick is timing the hedge work around the busy points in the town's calendar: the Dickens Festival in June, the August bank holiday and the summer changeover days, while keeping to the rules on nesting birds.

The fixed points in the Broadstairs calendar

Broadstairs Dickens Festival: held every June. Holiday lets in the town tend to be busy for the festival, and a tired front hedge shows up in guest photos and reviews.

August bank holiday weekend: one of the busiest weekends of the summer in a seaside town. Owners usually want the cleaner and the front-of-house tidy finished in the short gap between guest checkout and check-in, so a hedge tidy needs to be a quick in-and-out job, booked ahead.

School holiday changeovers: Fridays and Saturdays through July and August are the usual changeover days. A tidy hedge matters at listing-photograph time and again when guests arrive. I would book the tidy for the day before the changeover rather than on the morning itself, when the road and driveway are at their busiest.

The nesting-law complication

The awkward bit. The peak dates, the Dickens Festival in June, the August bank holiday and the July school holidays, all fall inside the 1 March to 31 August window that RSPB and GOV.UK guidance treats as the main bird nesting season. The Wildlife & Countryside Act 1981 makes it an offence to intentionally or recklessly take, damage or destroy a nest while it is in use or being built, and to injure or kill a wild bird. On summary conviction the penalty is up to six months in prison, an unlimited fine, or both, with the fine assessed for each nest. So a hedge that hasn't been touched since early spring should not simply be hard-cut in June for kerb appeal without a careful check.

The workaround that keeps you on the right side of the law

Book a pre-season cut early, before nesting is at its peak. Then keep to light top-and-face tidies in June and August on a hedge that has been checked and found clear: no hard reductions, no base work, nothing that disturbs an active nest. If I find a nest in use, that part of the hedge is left alone until the birds have finished. The hedge stays sharp through the busy weeks and the nest check is how you avoid committing the offence.

What the tidy actually looks like

A holiday-let front-hedge tidy is not the same job as an annual maintenance cut. It is a short visit with small-scale kit, and specifically:

  • Face-clip on the street-facing side, sharp defined lines. Top-clip level.
  • Retrieve fallen debris around the base. Wind on the cliff-top tends to push general litter into the base of Escallonia and Griselinia hedges.
  • Sweep the pavement.
  • Leave the rear-facing side and interior alone unless there is a clear cosmetic issue from inside the property.
  • Arisings bagged and taken away the same day. On a tight turnaround, a garden waste bag left on the pavement between checkout and check-in defeats the point.

Not a lawn cut, not a border tidy, not a full annual. The point is good kerb appeal for the guest arrival, and that is a targeted job with a specific spec.

Access and scheduling on a changeover day

Practical points for holiday-let owners:

  • Get in before the cleaner arrives. Book the hedge for the day before, or early on the changeover morning, so the driveway and access are clear.
  • Key access. Owners with a smart-lock code or a key safe make this a much cleaner operation. Meeting on site to hand over a key on a busy changeover morning is a false economy.
  • Parking. Many streets near the town centre are permit or pay-and-display, so it helps to tell me where a van can safely stop for the length of the visit.
  • Waste route. Thanet District Council's garden waste subscription (£73 a year for 2026/27) takes stems under 7.5cm, so a holiday-let fed with regular light trims can fit within it. For heavier material from your own property, Margate household waste recycling centre on Manston Road (Kent County Council, booking required before you visit) takes household garden waste; it does not accept trade waste. If I do the work, I take the arisings away and dispose of them at a licensed waste site.

Between-let plans vs one-offs

Two patterns work for holiday-let owners in Broadstairs; a third does not.

The seasonal plan. An early pre-season cut, a few light tidies through June to August timed to specific guest turnovers, and a firm cut to close the season in the autumn. I quote a fixed price for each visit after seeing photos, and booking the dates early gives the best chance of fitting the busy weeks.

The on-demand tidy. Message on WhatsApp a few days before a specific guest arrival. I will slot you in if the diary allows, but there is no guarantee in festival week or bank-holiday week. It works for smaller portfolios or owners who don't run the property year-round.

The "cheap annual". A single hard cut late in the season and nothing else. This is the pattern that tends to underperform: by high summer a coastal hedge has put on a lot of new growth, looks ragged in listing photographs, and guests notice. A small amount of regular light trimming usually costs less than the damage to a listing.
