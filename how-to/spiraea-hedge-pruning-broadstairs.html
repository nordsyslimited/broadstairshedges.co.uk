How to prune a spiraea hedge in Broadstairs: Japanese and spring-flowering types for CT10.

Spiraea is one of the most commonly planted informal hedging and edging shrubs in Broadstairs and St Peter's gardens, yet it is also one of the most often pruned at the wrong time or in the wrong way. The key mistake: treating all spiraea alike. Japanese spiraea (Spiraea japonica) and spring-flowering spiraea (such as Spiraea arguta or bridal wreath) have opposite pruning seasons, and cutting on the wrong calendar produces either a hedge stripped of its flowers for a whole year or a leggy plant with spent wood dominating the base. This guide sets out the identification step, then the correct two-cut routine for each type.

Method

Step 1: Identify your spiraea type before picking up any tool

Two groups of spiraea are common in CT10 gardens, and their pruning timing is opposite:

  • Japanese spiraea (Spiraea japonica) and its cultivars (Anthony Waterer, Gold Mound, Golden Princess, Goldflame, Little Princess, Nana): flowers from late June to August on shoots grown during the current season. The flowers are flat-topped pink, red or white clusters, and the foliage is often yellow, bronze or gold-tinted in spring. This is the most widely planted group as a low informal hedge or front-garden edging in Broadstairs.
  • Spring-flowering spiraea (Spiraea arguta, bridal wreath; Spiraea thunbergii, baby's breath spiraea; Spiraea x vanhouttei): flowers in April or May on stems grown the previous summer, producing cascades of small white flowers. Cutting it in early spring removes the flower buds and you lose the display entirely for that year.

If you are unsure which you have, note when it flowers. Flowers before May: spring-flowering type. Flowers after June: Japanese type. If it has not yet flowered in your garden, check the label or look up the cultivar name on the plant tag.

Step 2: Check the hedge for active bird nests

Both types of spiraea require a cut during part of the bird nesting season. Walk the hedge slowly from one end to the other, watching for adults flying repeatedly into the same section, alarm calls from within the shrub, or visible nest material. If you find an active nest with eggs or young birds, stop work on that section and return no earlier than when the chicks have left the nest.

Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981: what it means for spiraea pruning

Section 1 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 makes it an offence to intentionally or recklessly damage or destroy an active nest. The accepted caution window is 1 March to 31 August, and spiraea's post-flowering trim for Japanese types falls squarely within this window. Spring-flowering types pruned in late May or June are also inside the window. The early-spring renovation cut for Japanese spiraea (February to early March) is the one window that typically falls outside the season, which is why it is the safer structural cut. Penalty on summary conviction: an unlimited fine per nest. The nest check at Step 2 is not optional.

Step 3 (Japanese spiraea): early-spring structural cut, February to early March

For Spiraea japonica grown as a compact informal hedge, do the main structural work in late February or early March, before buds break. Use sharp bypass secateurs or hand shears. Cut all stems back to within 25-30cm of ground level. This sounds severe but the plant regenerates freely from the base and the new shoots carry the summer flowers. For a hedge that has been allowed to reach 80cm or more over several seasons without a hard cut, this single early-spring cut in the first year will bring it back to a manageable 60-70cm by August.

If you want a taller informal screen rather than a tight low edging, use a one-third renewal approach instead: remove the oldest one-third of stems entirely at ground level, shorten the remaining stems by about a third, and leave the youngest growth largely untouched. Repeat each February.

Step 4 (Japanese spiraea): post-flowering deadhead, July to August

Once the flowers fade in late July or August, deadhead by cutting the spent flower heads back to the first set of leaves beneath the cluster. Do not cut hard into old wood at this stage. The purpose of the post-flowering cut is to tidy the appearance, encourage any potential second flush of flowers on cultivars that rebloom (Anthony Waterer sometimes obliges), and reduce the energy spent on seed set. This is a light cut: 10-15cm off the tips, not a structural cut.

Remember: this cut is inside the 1 March-31 August window. Complete the nest check from Step 2 before starting.

Step 3 (spring-flowering spiraea): post-flowering cut, late May to June

For Spiraea arguta and other spring-blooming types, the only safe structural cut is immediately after flowering ends, typically late May or early June. Cut the stems that have just flowered back by about half, to a strong outward-facing bud or a young shoot growing from lower down. Do not leave the cut until midsummer: the plant needs time to grow the replacement shoots that will carry next year's flowers before the growth season ends. Remove any dead stems from the base entirely.

If the plant has become very leggy with most of the growth at the top and bare woody stems below, carry out a phased renovation: cut half the oldest stems hard to 20cm in year one immediately after flowering, cut the other half in year two. You will lose some flowering display in both years but the plant will recover its shape over two seasons.

Step 5: Annual stem renewal for both types

Each year, regardless of type, remove two or three of the oldest and thickest stems entirely at the base with loppers. Old stems become less productive over time and the interior of the hedge fills with spent wood. Removing the oldest ones annually keeps the centre open and the flowering wood young. On a plant that has been neglected for five years or more, you may find a thicket of old grey stems at the base: remove these over two seasons rather than in one cut to avoid shocking the plant.

Step 6: Clear clippings and mulch around the base

Gather all clippings from the ground. Spiraea clippings are fine and can go straight on the compost heap. Apply a 5-7cm mulch of well-rotted compost or bark around the base of the hedge, keeping the mulch away from the stems themselves. In CT10 gardens on thin chalk topsoil, this annual mulch makes a real difference: spiraea prefers a slightly acidic to neutral, moisture-retentive soil, and Margate chalk is the opposite. Building organic matter above the chalk improves water retention and nutrient availability. Repeat the mulch each spring.

Japanese vs spring-flowering spiraea: species notes for Broadstairs gardens

Spiraea japonica and its cultivars are the dominant spiraea in CT10 gardens, planted widely as a low informal hedge along front-garden boundaries, as edging to paths, and as a front-boundary softening plant in Victorian terraces around Broadstairs and St Peter's. The golden and bronze-leaved cultivars (Goldflame, Gold Mound, Golden Princess) are prized for spring foliage colour as much as for their July flowers. At mature size most cultivars reach 60-90cm in height and spread, which suits a low boundary role in smaller gardens.

Spring-flowering spiraea (bridal wreath, Spiraea arguta) is less common as a formal hedge and more often seen as a stand-alone specimen or mixed informal boundary planting. Its cascading white flowers are spectacular in April but the display is over in two to three weeks, after which the plant needs cutting to perform well the following year. Left unpruned, bridal wreath becomes a large, arching shrub 2-3m tall with most of the flowering wood at the outer tips and a dense tangle of old wood at the centre.

Spiraea on the Thanet coast: what changes near Broadstairs

Spiraea is not a front-line coastal species. It will not sit at the seaward edge of an exposed clifftop in the same way as Olearia, Escallonia or Tamarisk. The salt-wind tolerance is moderate: it performs well in sheltered front gardens and in the lee of a building or existing coastal hedge, but a south-east facing slope directly above the Broadstairs or St Peter's cliffs would scorch the new foliage each spring. Position it as a second or third line if you are working with a tiered coastal planting scheme.

Low-growing cultivars handle the exposure better than tall ones. Little Princess and Nana, both staying under 60cm, are more wind-tolerant than the 90cm Anthony Waterer in fully exposed positions. The foliage on golden varieties tends to be slightly more prone to salt scorch on cut surfaces, so do the post-flowering deadhead with secateurs rather than a hedge trimmer to reduce the number of cut edges exposed to the sea wind.

On the chalk topsoil that covers much of North Foreland, Kingsgate and Reading Street, the annual compost mulch from Step 6 is especially important. Spiraea planted directly into thin chalk without any soil improvement will establish slowly and flower poorly. Work in at least a bucketful of well-rotted compost per plant at planting time and follow up with the annual mulch thereafter.

Renovating a neglected spiraea hedge in CT10

A spiraea hedge that has not been cut for several years typically presents as a thicket of old woody stems 1-1.5m tall with a sparse crown of flowering tips. Unlike Leylandii or some laurels, spiraea regenerates freely from the base, so hard renovation is straightforward. For Japanese spiraea: cut all stems back to 20-30cm in late February, apply a generous mulch and a slow-release fertiliser (a handful of blood, fish and bone per plant worked into the mulch), and water in during any dry spring spells. By July the plant will have produced vigorous new growth and will carry flowers. The hedge will look bare for the first 6-8 weeks after the cut but recovers quickly once the growing season starts.

For spring-flowering spiraea: carry out hard renovation immediately after flowering ends in late May or June. The recovery takes longer because the plant must produce a full season of new growth before it can flower again. Expect a reduced or absent flower display the following spring, with full recovery by the spring two years after the renovation cut.
