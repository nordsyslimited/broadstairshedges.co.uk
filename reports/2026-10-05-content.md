## Broadstairs/Thanet specificity

The article incorporates the following localising detail:
- Margate Chalk Formation: porous, alkaline (pH 7.5+), topsoil typically 100-300mm over solid chalk, slow organic-matter decomposition
- Local old orchard/market-garden history in St Peter's and Reading Street as a source of honey fungus inoculum in garden soil
- Street-level specificity: Albion Road, Pierremont Avenue, St Peter's Park Road as typical Victorian terrace privet-hedge locations
- CT10 chalk depth limitation: rhizomorph barrier installation limited to 200-300mm (chalk bedrock constraint, not the usual 500mm)
- Species note: privet is the most commonly killed species in UK gardens by honey fungus, and it is ubiquitous as a boundary hedge in Broadstairs terraces
- Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 nesting-season callout placed at Step 5 (removal of infected plants), noting the September-February window as a safer removal timing
