## Broadstairs/Thanet specificity

The article incorporates the following localising detail:

- **Victorian and Edwardian context:** ceanothus specimens in Victorian/Edwardian terrace streets behind Viking Bay and around St Peter's named as a specific CT10 distribution pattern. 'Gloire de Versailles' named as the deciduous cultivar most common in Victorian-era British garden schemes.
- **Margate Chalk Formation:** alkaline (pH 7.5-8.0), fast-draining; directly relevant to ceanothus preference for well-drained, relatively low-fertility soils. Free-draining chalk noted as matching ceanothus's native Californian coastal soils more closely than heavier UK clay soils.
- **Annual rainfall:** approximately 615mm in CT10, one of England's drier areas, noted as an advantage for ceanothus establishment and ongoing health.
- **Named locations:** Viking Bay terraces, St Peter's, Kingsgate clifftop gardens, North Foreland gardens. Sheltered south/west-facing wall positions in these areas identified as the best-performing sites.
- **North Sea exposure risk:** salt-laden easterly winds and wet-cold frost conditions identified as the key CT10 risk for ceanothus, distinct from drought (which is the main risk in inland gardens).
- **Conservation area detail:** four named CAs (Broadstairs CA, St Peter's CA, Reading Street CA, Kingsgate CA), section 211 TCPA 1990 threshold (75mm at 1.5m), practical guidance that normal pruning does not trigger it.
- **Cultivar-specific notes:** 'Concha' named as widely planted in the Victorian terrace streets; 'Puget Blue' named in Kingsgate clifftop gardens; 'Autumnal Blue' identified as hardier and more tolerant of exposure.
