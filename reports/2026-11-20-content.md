**Gap rationale:** A survey of all existing how-to articles confirmed no article covering Chaenomeles or flowering quince existed. Chaenomeles is one of the most widely planted wall shrubs in older Broadstairs and St Peter's front gardens: the wall-trained japonica against a south- or west-facing fence is a characteristic feature of Victorian and interwar housing stock throughout CT10. The pruning requirement is specific: the plant flowers on wood made the previous year, so timing the cut (immediately after flowering) is non-negotiable, and wall-trained plants require a two-pass spur-pruning regime to maintain flower cover and flat structure. The nesting-season consideration is real and falls squarely within the main pruning window. Category covered: Cutting technique (also touches Wildlife and timing).

- Drought tolerance noted with specific Manston rainfall figure (615mm/yr).
